## Q1. Taylor & Greve (2006, AMJ), "Superman or the Fantastic Four?": solo creators vs teams, diverse genre experience, mean vs variance of outcomes

### Takeaway
- **Citation verified:** Alva Taylor & Henrich R. Greve, AMJ 49(4): 723–740 (2006), comic-book industry.
- **Core result:** knowledge from several domains (genres) produces novel combinations that raise the *variance* of product performance, so more hits and more flops. Extensive experience produces *high average* performance.
- **Solo vs team:** individual creators combined diverse knowledge *more effectively than teams*. Per Greve, individuals with experience in more than three genres ended up more innovative than teams, even though individuals start out less innovative.

### Cited Findings
- **Citation details.** Taylor, A. & Greve, H. R. (2006), "Superman or the Fantastic Four? Knowledge Combination and Experience in Innovative Teams," *Academy of Management Journal* 49(4), pp. 723–740, DOI 10.5465/AMJ.2006.22083029. The setting is comic books. — [INSEAD publication listing](https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/superman-or-fantastic-four-knowledge-combination-and); [SUFE academic newsletter listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087); [Fundación MAPFRE index record](https://documentacion.fundacionmapfre.org/documentacion/en/bib/85088.do)
- **Core theory** (abstract wording as returned by search): "multiple knowledge domains produce novel combinations that increase the variance of product performance and that extensive experience produces outputs with high average performance." — [INSEAD listing](https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/superman-or-fantastic-four-knowledge-combination-and); [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087)
- **Key solo-vs-team result** (abstract wording as returned by search): "individuals were able to combine knowledge diversity more effectively than teams." — [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087); [INSEAD listing](https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/superman-or-fantastic-four-knowledge-combination-and)
- **Team result, PARAPHRASE ONLY (exact abstract wording not retrieved):** the search engine's paraphrase of the abstract says teams, and team members' experience of working together, were associated with more variable innovation value. — [INSEAD listing](https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/superman-or-fantastic-four-knowledge-combination-and)
- **Greve's own later account:**
  - Experience across more than three genres made individual creators more innovative than teams, "even though individuals start out being less innovative."
  - Source: Greve's blog, September 2021 archive, as summarized by the search engine. — [Henrich Greve blog, 09-2021](https://www.henrichgreve.com/blog/archives/09-2021)
- **Popular discussion (content not retrieved):** a 2025 comics-criticism blog post titled "Genre Breadth Leads to Greatness?" discusses genre breadth among comics creators. — [Kleefeld on Comics, May 2025](https://www.kleefeldoncomics.com/2025/05/genre-breadth-leads-to-greatness.html)

### Inferences
- **Two separate outcome dimensions.**
  - Breadth (multi-genre experience) mainly moves the *spread* of outcomes; accumulated experience mainly moves the *average*.
  - The evidence-based answer to "is a jack-of-all-trades worse?" is therefore "breadth buys more lottery tickets for an outlier; depth and experience raise the floor and the mean." It is not "breadth is bad."
- **Most relevant to a solo developer:** "individuals combine knowledge diversity better than teams."
  - Integrating diverse knowledge inside one head can beat distributing it across a team.
  - The pay-off comes as higher variance, so failure is also more likely. Per Greve, individuals also start from a lower innovativeness baseline.
- **Construct caveat for the final report.**
  - "Breadth" here means experience across *content genres* (e.g., superhero vs horror vs humor). It does not mean one person performing several *production roles* (writing + pencilling + inking).
  - It should not be equated directly with "one person doing 3D modeling + animation + music + code."
- **Evidence type:** archival observational data, so the findings are correlational, not causal.
  - Creators who choose to work across many genres may differ in talent or ambition (self-selection).
  - So the "breadth → variance" link should not be read as causal.

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved:**
  - the exact sample (years; numbers of comics and creators);
  - the measure of "innovation value" (I recall a collector-market value measure, UNVERIFIED);
  - effect sizes, and the model used to estimate mean and variance separately.
- **Team findings unconfirmed:** the direction and wording of the team / joint-experience findings come only from a search paraphrase. Verify against the abstract or full text.
- **No replication or critique** of this specific study was found before the search budget ran out.

## Q2. Mannucci & Yong (2018, AMJ): data source and how depth and breadth effects change across career stages

### Takeaway
- **Citation verified:** Pier Vittorio Mannucci & Kevyn Yong, AMJ 61(5): 1741–1763 (2018).
- **Mechanism:** the creative value of depth versus breadth depends on career stage, because knowledge structures grow more rigid with career age.
- **Direction:** depth helps creativity most *early* in a career; breadth helps *later*.
- **Data:** per the search results, a longitudinal study of the **Hollywood animation industry**, not video games or comics.

### Cited Findings
- **Citation details.** Mannucci, P. V. & Yong, K. (2018), "The differential impact of knowledge depth and knowledge breadth on creativity over individual careers," *Academy of Management Journal* 61(5): 1741–1763, DOI 10.5465/amj.2016.0529. — [LBS Research Online record](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/917); [Bocconi IRIS record](https://iris.unibocconi.it/handle/11565/4046009); [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/360343)
- **Starting point:** prior research produced inconsistent findings on how depth and breadth affect creativity. The authors argue the effect depends on career stage, because knowledge structures grow more rigid as career age increases. — [LBS Research Online record](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/917)
- **Direction:** depth is most beneficial early in a career, when people need to build more complex knowledge structures. Breadth is most beneficial later, when flexibility matters more. — [LBS Research Online record](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/917); [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/360343)
- **Data (per search summary of the abstract):**
  - a longitudinal study of the Hollywood animation industry, a setting "characterized by a variety of creators involved in knowledge-intensive activities";
  - the hypotheses are reported as supported.
  - — [LBS Research Online record](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/917)
- **HEC Paris summary:** different kinds of knowledge matter at different career stages, and the study rejects the older view that creativity simply peaks mid-career. — [HEC Paris Knowledge](https://www.hec.edu/en/knowledge/instants/how-knowledge-can-stimulate-also-impede-creativity)
- **UK Enterprise Research Centre briefing (Friedrich):** describes the depth advantage as applying mainly to people who are newer to a field. — [ERC briefing No. 49](https://www.enterpriseresearch.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/No49-Building-a-creative-work-force-T.-Friedrich.pdf)
- **Likely working-paper version:** an ERIM seminar titled "To Infinity, and Beyond? Career Age, Cognitive Structures, and Creativity over Time" appeared in search results for this paper. Its Toy Story reference fits the animation setting (inference). — [ERIM event listing](https://erim.eur.nl/research/events/detail/3827)
- **LBS coverage:** London Business School's "Creativity revisited" article covers this line of research. — [LBS Think](https://www.london.edu/think/creativity-revisited)

### Inferences
- **Sequence for a novice:** for someone early in a domain (low career age), the evidence favours building depth first. Breadth pays off more once a deep base exists. This is a "depth first, breadth later" sequence, a T-shape built over time.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Rigidity (entrenchment) rises with career age, and breadth counteracts it later in a career.
  - Depth supplies the complex knowledge structures beginners lack.
  - "Career age" means time in the career, not biological age; the working-paper title uses "career age".
- **Evidence type:** longitudinal archival data on individual careers, so correlational. Career stage is not randomly assigned. Survivorship is likely: people who stay long enough to accumulate breadth may be the more capable ones.
- **External validity:**
  - Hollywood animation is a large-studio, team-based setting. "Breadth" there most likely means breadth of project, genre or role experience within studio careers.
  - It is not solo multi-skilling, and transfer to a solo indie game developer is analogical.

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved:**
  - sample size and years;
  - the creativity measure (e.g., critics' ratings or awards);
  - the exact operationalization of depth and breadth;
  - effect sizes and the functional form (e.g., interactions with career age).
- **Data source to confirm:** "Hollywood animation" rests on the search summary of the LBS abstract. Confirm against the full text.
- **Affiliations unverified:** one search summary said both authors were at ESSEC; the HEC page suggests Yong was at HEC Paris. This does not affect the findings.
- **No critiques or replications** were found before the search budget ran out.

## Q3. Teodoridis, Bikard & Vakili (2019, ASQ): when do specialists vs generalists produce more creative work?

### Takeaway
- **Citation verified:** ASQ 64(4): 894–927 (2019).
- **Design:** the authors use the collapse of the Soviet Union as a natural experiment. It suddenly moved the knowledge frontier in some subfields of theoretical mathematics more than in others.
- **Slow-paced domains:** generalists did relatively better.
- **Fast-paced domains:** specialists gained the edge, producing more papers and more breakthroughs, and became more sought-after collaborators.

### Cited Findings
- **Citation details.** Florenta Teodoridis, Michaël Bikard & Keyvan Vakili, "Creativity at the Knowledge Frontier: The Impact of Specialization in Fast- and Slow-paced Domains," *Administrative Science Quarterly* 64(4): 894–927, DOI 10.1177/0001839218793384. — [SAGE figshare supplemental material](https://sage.figshare.com/articles/DS_10_1177_0001839218793384_Supplemental_material_for_Creativity_at_the_Knowledge_Frontier_The_Impact_of_Specialization_in_Fast-_and_Slow-paced_Domains_/6881681/1); [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/996); [London Business School listing](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/c/creativity-at-the-knowledge-frontier-the-impact-of-specialization-in-fast-and-slow-paced-domains)
- **Earlier working-paper title:** "The Pace of Change and Creative Performance: Specialist and Generalist Mathematicians at the Fall of the Soviet Union." — [SSRN 2911141](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2911141); [Berkeley SIH working-paper PDF](https://sih.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/The-Pace-of-Change-and-Creative-Performance.pdf)
- **Framing:** past disagreement about whether specialists or generalists are more creative may stem from how a domain's *pace of change* shapes the relative returns to each profile. — [SSRN 2911141](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2911141)
- **Design:** the Soviet collapse "moved the frontier in some domains of theoretical mathematics more than in others," and the study treats this as a natural experiment. — [SSRN 2911141](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2911141); [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/996)
- **Findings:**
  - Generalists did best when change was slow, because they could draw on diverse domains.
  - Specialists gained the edge when change sped up, because deeper expertise let them exploit new knowledge at the frontier.
  - Specialists became more desirable collaborators in faster-changing fields, generalists in slower ones.
  - Specialists in the fastest-advancing domains produced more publications and more breakthroughs than generalists.
  - — [SSRN 2911141](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2911141); [INSEAD Knowledge, "Creating fast and slow"](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/creating-fast-and-slow)
- **Magnitude (INDICATIVE ONLY):**
  - Per the INSEAD Knowledge article as returned by search, specialists published 83% more papers than generalists in 1990–2000, relative to the ten years before the collapse, "after weighting the data."
  - Which domains this covers, and how the weighting worked, could not be checked.
  - — [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/creating-fast-and-slow)
- **Bikard's framing (in a 2025 INSEAD piece, per search):** specialists perform relatively better as the pace of change increases because their narrow focus lets them go deeper. Creative strategies involve trade-offs rather than one profile being superior. — [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/creating-fast-and-slow)

### Inferences
- **Strongest verified pro-specialist evidence, but bounded.**
  - This is the strongest verified evidence that specialists can outperform.
  - It comes with an explicit boundary condition: a *fast-moving knowledge frontier*. In slow-moving domains the advantage shifts to generalists.
- **Strength of evidence:**
  - The shock to the pace of change is plausibly exogenous, so the design is quasi-experimental: specialists' and generalists' outcomes are compared before and after, across differently affected domains.
  - Specialization itself is self-selected, so the causal claim concerns *pace moderating the specialist premium*, not randomly assigned specialization.
- **Applying it to indie game development (SPECULATIVE; the study does not test this):** one would need to judge which parts of the work are fast-paced frontiers.
  - Engine and AI tooling change fast.
  - Character-animation principles, music fundamentals and game-design craft change slowly.
- **Population gap:** the population is elite theoretical mathematicians (publication outcomes). That is far from commercial creative products judged by audiences.

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved:**
  - sample size;
  - how specialist vs generalist was measured (probably concentration of publications across math subfields, UNVERIFIED);
  - how breakthroughs were measured;
  - coefficients and confidence intervals.
- **No published critiques or replications** were found before the search budget ran out.

## Q4. Fleming (2007, MIT SMR) and Fleming, Mingo & Chen (2007, ASQ): does combining distant knowledge raise variance (more breakthroughs AND more failures)?

### Takeaway
- **What could be verified:** only the existence and abstract of the MIT SMR article.
  - It argues that invention outcomes are highly skewed (a "long tail").
  - Managers should track "shots on goal, average score, and maximum score."
  - Invention is framed as recombinant search.
- **Specific variance claim NOT verified:** the claim that distant combinations lower the mean but raise variance could not be confirmed from retrieved text.
- **Fleming, Mingo & Chen (2007) NOT verified:** its content could not be confirmed, and from memory it may not be primarily a variance study (see Gaps).
- **Related verified support:** Taylor & Greve's verified comic-book result (Q1), that multiple knowledge domains raise performance variance, supports the general "breadth → variance" idea.

### Cited Findings
- **Citation details.** "Breakthroughs and the 'Long Tail' of Innovation," by Lee Fleming, *MIT Sloan Management Review*. — [MIT SMR shop listing](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/products/49114); [alternate MIT SMR listing](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/breakthroughs-and-the-long-tail-of-innovation)
- **Abstract claim:** "the largely erroneous perception that breakthroughs are impossible to predict arises from the tendency to focus on just the breakthroughs while ignoring the iterative process of invention and its distribution of outcomes." — [MIT SMR shop listing](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/products/49114)
- **Article content (per search summary of the abstract and opening):**
  - Outcomes are strongly skewed: most ideas add little value and a handful become breakthroughs, the "long tail."
  - Fleming proposes tracking "shots on goal, average score, and maximum score."
  - Invention is recombinant search, a new combination of existing components, ideas or processes; examples include the steamship and the iPod.
  - — [MIT SMR shop listing](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/products/49114)
- **Author confirmed:** Lee Fleming (UC Berkeley). — [UC Berkeley IEOR faculty page](https://ieor.berkeley.edu/people/lee-fleming/)
- **Related verified evidence from comics:** "multiple knowledge domains produce novel combinations that increase the variance of product performance." — [SUFE listing of Taylor & Greve 2006](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087)

### Inferences
- **Even without the specific variance numbers, the framework applies:**
  - The best outcome of a creative portfolio depends on the *number of attempts* (shots on goal) and the *spread* of outcomes, not only the average.
  - A solo developer betting three years on one game takes essentially one shot. Smaller prototypes, game jams or smaller releases add shots.
- **Consistency:** "distant recombination → higher variance" is consistent with the verified comic-book evidence (Q1). In the verified evidence, it is tied to outcome variance, not to a higher mean.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED for the MIT SMR article:** any numbers in its full text; publication date and issue. Product code 49114 is consistent with vol. 49, no. 1, Fall 2007, but that is an inference.
- **UNVERIFIED LEAD: Fleming, Mingo & Chen (2007, ASQ), "Collaborative brokerage, generative creativity, and creative success."**
  - Recalled finding: inventors in brokerage positions generate more new combinations, but those combinations are less likely to be taken up and used by others; cohesive collaboration networks show the opposite.
  - If correct, the paper is about generation vs diffusion in networks, not primarily about outcome variance. The user's framing may conflate it with other Fleming papers.
- **UNVERIFIED LEADS likely to be the true sources of the "variance" claim (verify before citing):**
  - Fleming (2001), *Management Science*, "Recombinant uncertainty in technological search." Recalled: recombining unfamiliar components or new combinations raises the uncertainty/variability of how useful inventions turn out to be.
  - Fleming (2004), *Harvard Business Review*, "Perfecting cross-pollination." Recalled: inventions from inventors or teams spanning distant fields have lower average value but higher variance, including the most valuable breakthroughs.
- **UNVERIFIED LEADS on lone inventors, highly relevant to solo development (verify):**
  - Singh & Fleming (2010), *Management Science*, "Lone inventors as sources of breakthroughs: myth or reality?" Recalled: lone inventors are *less* likely to produce breakthroughs and *more* likely to produce very poor outcomes; collaboration helps through both idea diversity and stricter selection.
  - Dahlin, Taylor & Fichman (2004), *Research Policy*, "Today's Edisons or weekend hobbyists." Recalled: independent inventors are over-represented at *both* extremes of technical importance.
  - These two may point in different directions and need reconciling after verification.

## Q5. Video game industry research: de Vaan, Stark & Vedres (2015); Mollick (2012); role breadth, solo/small teams, generalists vs specialists in studios

### Takeaway
- **de Vaan, Stark & Vedres (2015, AJS; 12,422 games, 139,727 developers):** game-changing success is more likely when teams draw on overlapping cohesive groups ("structural folding"), especially when those groups are cognitively distant (different stylistic/genre backgrounds).
- **Mollick (2012, SMJ; 854 games, more than US$4bn revenue):** variation among *producers* (middle managers who integrate) explains much more performance variation than variation among *designers* (innovative roles).
  - The practitioner essay version gives over 22% vs about 7%.
- **Gap:** no academic study of role breadth among solo developers or small teams could be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **de Vaan et al., citation details.** de Vaan, M., Stark, D. & Vedres, B. (2015), "Game Changer: The Topology of Creativity," *American Journal of Sociology* 120(4): 1144–1194. — [CEU research portal](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/game-changer-the-topology-of-creativity-2/)
  - Italian translation listed on rivisteweb. — [rivisteweb](https://www.rivisteweb.it/doi/10.1425/78526)
  - Russian translation in *Journal of Economic Sociology* 17(4): 53–99 (2016). — [HSE](https://ecsoc.hse.ru/en/2016-17-4/192204467.html)
- **de Vaan et al., data:**
  - Team reassembly across 12,422 video games and the career histories of 139,727 developers. — [CEU research portal](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/game-changer-the-topology-of-creativity-2/)
  - A Stark seminar abstract gives a slightly different count: 12,094 games produced from the industry's start in 1979 to 2009, probably an earlier sample version. — [CBS seminar listing](https://www.cbs.dk/node/254873); [RUG colloquium listing](https://rug.nl/news/2012/11/object1502548519)
- **de Vaan et al., concept and hypothesis:**
  - "Structural folding" means members of one cohesive group overlapping with another cohesive group.
  - Its effect on game-changing success is hypothesized to be especially strong when the overlapping groups are cognitively distant.
  - — [CEU research portal](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/game-changer-the-topology-of-creativity-2/)
- **de Vaan et al., finding:** "When combined with cognitive distance, structural folding channels and mobilizes a productive tension of rules, roles, and codes that promotes successful innovation." — [CEU research portal](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/game-changer-the-topology-of-creativity-2/)
- **Mollick, citation details.** Mollick, E. (2012), "People and Process, Suits and Innovators: The Role of Individuals in Firm Performance," *Strategic Management Journal* 33(9): 1001–1015. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v33y2012i9p1001-1015.html); [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/mgmt_papers/34)
- **Mollick, method:** a Multiple Membership Cross-Classified multilevel model of 854 computer games, together accounting for over $4 billion in revenue. It asks how much of performance variance comes from individual members rather than the organization. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v33y2012i9p1001-1015.html); [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/mgmt_papers/34)
- **Mollick, finding (abstract):**
  - Individual variation matters more than usually assumed.
  - Variation among middle managers (producers) has a particularly large impact on firm performance, "much larger than that of those individuals who are assigned innovative roles" (designers).
  - — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v33y2012i9p1001-1015.html)
- **Mollick, magnitudes (from his practitioner essay "Why producers matter (statistically) more than designers," via a Chinese repost):**
  - Sample: 854 games, 537 producers, 739 designers, 395 companies, 1994–2006.
  - After controls, differences among individual producers accounted for "over 22%" of games' financial success; differences among designers about 7%.
  - Producer effects were portable when producers moved between developers.
  - Mollick calls the result "extremely robust."
  - — [GamerBoom repost (Chinese translation)](https://gamerboom.com/archives/32181)
- **Mollick, related dissertation:** a related MIT dissertation record describes the middle-manager result in similar terms. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/57975)
- **Other game-industry studies surfaced by search (content NOT retrieved; leads only):**
  - Lê, Massé & Paris, "Technological change at the heart of the creative process: insights from the videogame industry." — [IP Paris research portal](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/technological-change-at-the-heart-of-the-creative-process-insight/)
  - "Generation and Evaluation in the Human Invention Process through the Lens of Game Design" (arXiv 2508.10914, 2025). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10914)
  - A Cranfield thesis, "Mapping the process of product innovation: contextualising the 'black box' of computer and video games design." — [Cranfield DSpace](https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/handle/1826/4154)
  - Tampere University, "Idea practices and attitudes towards innovation in game development: a snapshot from the 2010 mobile games era." — [Tampere research portal](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/idea-practices-and-attitudes-towards-innovation-in-game-developme/)
  - O'Donnell, *Developer's Dilemma: The Secret World of Videogame Creators* (MIT Press, 2014), an ethnography of game developers. — [New Books Network](https://newbooksnetwork.com/casey-odonnell-developers-dilemma-the-secret-world-of-videogame-creators-mit-press-2014)

### Inferences
- **Diversity plus structure (de Vaan et al.):**
  - In games, the benefit of recombining cognitively distant (diverse genre/style) experience appears *together with* a social structure of overlapping cohesive groups. The abstract does not show diversity alone to be sufficient.
  - This is a team/network finding and says nothing direct about a lone developer.
- **Integrators matter most (Mollick):**
  - The person in the integration/coordination role, who holds scope, schedule, people and parts together, is associated with more performance variance than the person in the "ideas" role.
  - For a solo developer, who is also their own producer, integration and project-management capacity may matter more than design brilliance.
  - This is an analogy: the data are mostly team-built commercial PC/console games from the 1994–2006 retail era.
- **Evidence type (both studies are observational):**
  - Mollick's study is a variance decomposition. It separates individual effects by relying on people who move across projects and firms. It shows how much variance is *associated* with the person in a role, not which skills cause it.
  - de Vaan et al. is a network-structural, correlational analysis.

### Gaps
- **de Vaan et al., not retrieved:**
  - how "game-changing success" is measured (I recall stylistic distinctiveness of a game's genre combination plus critical acclaim, UNVERIFIED);
  - how cognitive distance is measured (probably genre-experience profiles of groups, UNVERIFIED);
  - effect sizes.
- **Mollick, not retrieved:** the exact variance shares *in the SMJ paper itself*. The 22% and 7% come from the essay version.
- **UNVERIFIED LEADS (verify before citing):**
  - Zhao, Ishihara, Jennings & Lounsbury (2018), *Organization Science*, "Optimal distinctiveness in the console video game industry: an exemplar-based model of proto-type dynamics." Recalled: game sales depend on distinctiveness relative to genre exemplars, i.e., a game-level novelty–sales relationship.
  - Tschang (2007), *Organization Science*, "Balancing the tensions between rationalization and creativity in the video games industry." Qualitative.
  - Cennamo, Ozalp & Kretschmer (2018), *Information Systems Research*. Recalled: games released on more platforms can suffer quality trade-offs; a firm-level "spreading thin" analog.
- **KEY GAP for the user's case:** no study could be found or verified (searches exhausted) on:
  - the role breadth of solo/indie developers;
  - generalist vs specialist roles inside studios as predictors of success;
  - Steam-era indie team size vs outcomes.

## Q6. Other evidence on the "jack of all trades" question in creative careers (music, film, writers, designers), especially evidence that specialists outperform

### Takeaway
- **Little could be verified beyond Q1–Q5** this session.
- **Verified evidence that specialists outperform is conditional:**
  - specialists win at fast-moving frontiers (Teodoridis et al. 2019);
  - depth matters most early in a career (Mannucci & Yong 2018);
  - experience raises average quality (Taylor & Greve 2006).
- **Many relevant studies remain UNVERIFIED LEADS:** a large body of film, music, labor-market and invention research. They are listed in Gaps with the findings as I recall them, for a follow-up pass.

### Cited Findings
- **Specialists win at fast-moving frontiers:** specialists outperform generalists in fast-moving knowledge domains (more papers and breakthroughs; more sought-after as collaborators); generalists do better in slow-paced domains (mathematics, 2019). — [SSRN, Teodoridis et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2911141); [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/creating-fast-and-slow)
- **Depth early, breadth later:** depth helps creativity most early in careers; breadth helps more later (Hollywood animation, 2018). — [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/917)
- **Experience raises the mean, breadth raises variance:** extensive experience produces high average performance, while multi-domain knowledge raises variance; individuals integrate diverse knowledge better than teams (comics, 2006). — [SUFE listing of Taylor & Greve](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087)
- **Integrators matter more than idea roles:** in games, variation among producers (integrators) explains more revenue variance than variation among designers (2012). — [IDEAS/RePEc, Mollick 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v33y2012i9p1001-1015.html)

### Inferences
- **The absolute claim is not supported.** The verified studies do not support "today only specialists can succeed." They support conditional statements:
  - specialists have an edge at fast-changing technical frontiers and early in careers;
  - experience raises the floor;
  - breadth adds variance and novelty;
  - integration roles matter a great deal.
- **Two meanings of "breadth" to keep apart in the final report:**
  - (a) breadth of *knowledge, genre or domain experience*, which is what most of these studies measure;
  - (b) breadth of *roles or functions* performed by one person (artist + animator + composer + programmer), which the verified studies barely address.

## Q7. Synthesis: when does breadth help vs hurt creative success, and does the evidence support a T-shaped pattern?

### Takeaway
- **No universal winner.** The verified evidence points to a mean–variance trade-off plus conditions of timing and context.
- **Breadth (diverse domain/genre knowledge):**
  - raises novelty and the variance of outcomes;
  - helps in recombination and integration roles, in slow-moving domains, and later in a career.
- **Depth and experience:**
  - raise average quality;
  - win early in a career and at fast-moving frontiers.
- **T-shape:** a sequential "deep first, then broad" pattern (a T built over time) fits the verified findings. Direct tests of the T-shape remain unverified leads.

### Cited Findings
- **Breadth raises variance; experience raises the mean** (comics, 2006). — [SUFE listing of Taylor & Greve](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087)
- **Individuals integrate knowledge diversity better than teams.**
  - Per Greve, individuals with more than three genres of experience became more innovative than teams despite starting lower (2006 study; 2021 blog).
  - — [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/362087); [Greve blog](https://www.henrichgreve.com/blog/archives/09-2021)
- **Depth helps early, breadth later** (animation careers, 2018). — [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/917)
- **Pace decides the winner:** at a fast-moving frontier specialists win; in slow-paced domains generalists do better (mathematics; natural experiment; 2019). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2911141); [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/creating-fast-and-slow)
- **Diverse backgrounds need structure:** cognitive distance plus structural folding goes with game-changing success (video games, 2015). — [CEU research portal](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/game-changer-the-topology-of-creativity-2/)
- **Integrators explain more variance:** producers explain more performance variance than designers (video games, 2012). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v33y2012i9p1001-1015.html); [GamerBoom repost of Mollick essay](https://gamerboom.com/archives/32181)
- **Outcomes are long-tailed:** manage by shots on goal, average score and maximum score (2007). — [MIT SMR listing](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/products/49114)

### Inferences
- **When breadth helps (verified set):**
  - when the goal is a novel or distinctive product and high variance is acceptable (Taylor & Greve; de Vaan et al.);
  - in slow-changing knowledge domains (Teodoridis et al.);
  - later in a career, once depth exists (Mannucci & Yong);
  - in integration roles that coordinate many parts (Mollick, by analogy);
  - when the combining happens inside one person rather than across a team (Taylor & Greve).
- **When breadth hurts, or depth wins (verified set):**
  - early in a career (Mannucci & Yong);
  - at fast-moving technical frontiers (Teodoridis et al.);
  - when the priority is a reliable average and execution quality rather than a long-shot hit (Taylor & Greve: experience raises the mean).
  - Penalties from audience categorization (Hsu; Hsu et al.; Zuckerman et al.) are a plausible further condition but are UNVERIFIED here.
- **T-shape:**
  - No verified study directly tests "deep in one area, broad in others" at a single point in time.
  - The verified studies are consistent with a *temporal* T: depth first, breadth layered on later.
  - They are also consistent with a *portfolio* logic: experience and depth for the mean, breadth for the upper tail.
- **On the user's beliefs (all inference):**
  - **"People who do many things never go deep":** no verified study tests this directly. The binding constraint is the time budget.
    - Over three years, the evidence fits depth in one core craft plus "good enough" breadth elsewhere.
    - For a character-animation-centred game, the obvious core craft is character animation.
  - **"Today only specialists succeed":** the verified evidence makes this conditional (fast frontiers, early careers), not universal.
    - Most studied settings are team-based industries, where specialists succeed *inside teams that supply the breadth*.
    - A solo developer must supply that breadth personally, buy it, or substitute for it (asset stores, AI tools, collaborators).
- **Variance logic for the user's plan:**
  - Breadth combined with a single 3-year project is a high-variance bet with one draw.
  - Two hedges are consistent with the evidence: more draws (smaller releases or prototypes), or a higher mean (deep core skill, tight scope control, producer-style discipline).
